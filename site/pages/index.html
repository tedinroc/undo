Undo for Bitcoin

A cancel button for Bitcoin.

Your Bitcoin key is stolen. Bitcoin refuses that key alone. A public NEAR contract makes the thief wait, and your phone can cancel first.

The second signature comes from NEAR Chain Signatures, when that contract asks. NEAR can refuse; it can't take. If NEAR goes away, your cold key gets the coins out after a timelock.

Watch a recorded run

A replay of the final build's recorded runs, with their real hashes. Test coins only, on Mutinynet and NEAR testnet.

Try a cancel yourself: one browser with a passkey and two tabs, on a phone or a computer, or a phone and a laptop. A relay pays the NEAR gas, and its daily limit is shared, so it can be full.

Why not 2-of-3?

If one key is stolen and nobody is watching, 2-of-3 is safer: that thief gets nothing, and Undo's thief gets the coins after the wait. Undo makes a different trade. Spending needs no second device or hardware, so it can be used for everyday spending; a public contract controls the second signature, not a company; a cold key leaves alone if NEAR is gone. If you can keep two keys in reach for every spend, use 2-of-3.

What the queue shows

The queue is public: anyone can read each waiting request's amount and destination, before Bitcoin moves. A thief watching the queue sees the same. There is no privacy claim.

Status, with no page and no key

The contract says its own state in plain words. Paste this into a terminal:

curl -s https://test.rpc.fastnear.com -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"jsonrpc":"2.0","id":"u","method":"query","params":{"request_type":"call_function","finality":"final","account_id":"<CONTRACT>","method_name":"status","args_base64":"e30="}}' | python3 -c 'import sys,json;r=json.load(sys.stdin);x=r.get("result") or {};print(bytes(x["result"]).decode() if "result" in x else "RPC refused: "+json.dumps(r.get("error") or x.get("error")))'

<CONTRACT> is the contract this page names in its config.
